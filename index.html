I live and work in Vancouver, on Coast Salish Territories, British Columbia, Canada. Before that — and alongside it — I travelled the planet, and spent years in the bush: as a contractor, as a guide, and for reasons harder to put on a résumé. To discover myself. To be humbled by ground that does not care what title you bring.

As co-founder and principal of two enterprises, I built companies whose purpose was not merely to operate within land-management systems as they stood, but to shift the values those systems reward — across British Columbia and Western Canada — toward practices that take seriously the interdependence of ecological health, human livelihood, and the long horizon. That work has meant holding a vision large enough to reframe what “good management” is for, and carrying it into rooms where power, capital, and obligation meet: shaping strategy at the edge of decision, and translating between field reality and the language of institutions.

I write as a humanist with a futurist’s patience — convinced that the measure of any system is whether people and places can still flourish inside it, generations on.
