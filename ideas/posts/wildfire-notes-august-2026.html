What’s new in wildfire

16 August 2026 · Shelby

Mid-August 2026. A weekly collation of the wildfire situation — Canada first, then the United States and the wider season. Figures are operational estimates from 15–16 August and will move. Names and maps below link to the agency sources.

Canada

The CIFFC Situation Report for 15 August puts the National Preparedness Level at 5 — full commitment of national resources, extreme demand for interagency support, limited national availability, and international resources being mobilized. Potential for significant new fires remains high to extreme in the active areas. Year to date from 1 January: about 4,730 fires and about 4.10 million hectares burned. Sixteen new fires were reported on 14 August, mostly in B.C. Hundreds of fires remain active across full-response, modified, and monitored categories, concentrated in the Northwest Territories, B.C., Ontario, and Quebec. See the CWFIS interactive map for hotspots, danger, and perimeters.

Area burned year to date is led by the Northwest Territories (~1.40 million ha), then Quebec (~0.75 million ha), Ontario (~0.73 million ha), and B.C. (~0.42 million ha). Alberta is under 18,000 ha. Other provinces and territories are much lower.

Hottest jurisdictions

Northwest Territories. Highest area burned — about 1.40 million hectares year to date, on the order of 211–277 fires, many active, out of control, or monitored. Large remote fires dominate; few recent new starts. NWT wildfire update and live map.

British Columbia. Most active now. About 130 active fires as of 16 August reports; about 420,000 ha year to date; provincial preparedness level 5. Significant fires include Pear Lake (~159,000 ha), Ainslie Creek, and Bald Range. Major evacuations earlier in August; a province-wide state of emergency was declared around 8 August. Multiple out-of-control fires and fires of note remain in the Interior, Coastal, Kamloops, and Southeast centres. Evacuations and area restrictions are still in effect in several locations. Fifteen new starts were reported on 14 August. Crews and aircraft are in from across Canada and abroad. BC Wildfire dashboard · map · provincial status.

Ontario. About 729 fires / 731,000 ha year to date. Northwestern focus: the Rinker Complex (Thunder Bay 36 ~301,000 ha plus Dryden fires — one of Ontario’s largest on record), the Atikokan cluster, and others. About 87 active recently in the northwest; some not under control. Evacuations persist (on the order of 800 still displaced recently; thousands have returned). Smoke reached southern Ontario and beyond earlier in the season. Ontario forest fires · interactive fire map.

Quebec. About 651 fires / 754,000 ha year to date. Activity declining; most under control or monitored. Resources being shared outward, including to B.C. Fire danger mostly low to moderate after recent precipitation. SOPFEU current situation · map.

Saskatchewan and Manitoba have significant year-to-date area (~291,000 / 264,000 ha) but lower current full-response activity; many fires are monitored or out. Alberta is at preparedness level 1 (~18,000 ha YTD). Atlantic provinces, Yukon, and Parks Canada are generally quieter, with some monitored or residual fire. International aid and domestic mutual aid are active. Air-quality impacts from smoke were notable earlier (including Toronto spikes in July); localized alerts continue near active fires.

United States

From NIFC National Fire News as of 15 August: 125 new fires the day before, including three new large fires; 88 uncontained large fires being worked; about 29,363 personnel assigned. Year to date: 48,617 fires / 7.20 million acres — well above the 10-year average of about 37,500 fires / 4.25 million acres. See also the NICC situation report (PDF) and InciWeb.

Large fires reported across Montana (6), Oregon (5), Colorado (4), Idaho (4), Washington (3), and others including New Mexico, California, Minnesota, South Dakota, Texas, and Utah. Many sit in the 1,000–9,000+ acre range with containment often under 50 percent. The Northwest and Great Basin are among the busier areas. Australian and New Zealand incident-management teams and fireline personnel are assigned to the Northwest and Great Basin; Canadian resources, including from Manitoba, are also assisting.

Fire weather: hot, dry, and windy conditions expected in the Southern Plains, critical in parts of Kansas, Missouri, Oklahoma, and Texas; thunderstorms possible in the Northwest and Rockies; watch also Puerto Rico, the Virgin Islands, and Hawaii (Hurricane Lala). California has ongoing large fires including Timber. Northwest states remain on community and infrastructure protection. NIFC maps · Cal Fire.

Global

NASA FIRMS (MODIS / VIIRS) shows near-real-time detections worldwide. Activity is seasonal and widespread. EFFIS as of about 13–14 August: roughly 568,000 ha burned year to date in the EU — below the 2025 peak, above the long-term average. Fire danger very high to extreme in parts of central-eastern Europe. Africa still holds the dominant share of global burned area (much of it agricultural and savanna). Australia / Oceania and the Amazon region show ongoing detections. Siberia and other Asian boreal zones are visible on FIRMS. Complementary products: GWIS current situation. North American activity — Canada plus the U.S. — is prominent in mid-August reporting.

Outlook

Canada at PL 5 reflects sustained pressure, especially B.C. (drought pockets, lightning risk) and residual large fires in the NWT and Ontario. Cooler or showery periods may stabilize some areas; dry fuels and thunderstorms keep risk elevated into late August. The U.S. remains on high personnel commitment, with critical fire-weather windows in the Plains and continued activity in the West. International resource sharing is active: Australia and New Zealand to the U.S., Canadian mutual aid, and support into B.C.

Evacuation orders, air-quality indexes (AQHI / AQI), and road closures sit with the local agencies linked above. Conditions change quickly.
