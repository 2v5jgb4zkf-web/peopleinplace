On opening this place

9 August 2026 · Shelby

Much of what we say in public is shaped by the room it enters. Short forms reward reaction; crowded platforms reward speed. Neither is especially good at holding an idea until it has edges — or at keeping a record of what is actually moving on the ground.

peopleinplace.ca is a quieter room of my own. It has two doors.

Ideas. One stream, newest first. Musings, thoughts, questions, and longer pieces — written in my own words, at their own length, outside corporate channels and social platforms. No forced shelves, no algorithm deciding what matters. Alongside those pieces sit periodic notes on what is moving in the fields I watch: ecological restoration, wildfire, the condition of land and the communities that live with it. The notes collate the public record and point to the primary sources, so anyone who wants to can go further on their own. I write from a humanist posture with a long horizon: people as the measure of the systems we build; place as the condition under which those systems either hold or fail.

Getting in touch. The other door. A private way to reach me — an old friend, a new opportunity, an inquiry, feedback, or something else on your mind. Leave a name, a way to reach you, and a note. It isn’t posted here. It comes only to me.

I have travelled the planet, and spent years in the bush — as a contractor, as a guide, and for reasons that never fit a job title: to discover myself, and to be humbled by country that does not negotiate with your sense of importance. That schooling sits under everything else.

My working life has also been that of a founder: co-owner and principal of two companies built to move land-management practice in British Columbia and Western Canada toward different values — ecological integrity held together with human thriving, not as afterthoughts to extraction or compliance. Building enterprises of that kind teaches you to see systems whole, to hold a vision longer than a funding cycle, and to speak in rooms where direction is set and the cost of short thinking is paid later, by other people, on other ground.

I live in Vancouver, on Coast Salish Territories, in British Columbia, Canada. That is the ground from which I write now. The site is a place to put thought when it is ready, to keep a public record of what is changing in the places that matter, and to be findable when someone is looking for more than a profile.

Not another network. A place to think in public, to follow what is moving — and a private door if you want to knock.

If you arrived from elsewhere, you are welcome to read. If we share a life or a labour and you have something to pass along, use the contact card.
